Why Kirkland streets get patched so poorly after builders dig them up for utility hookups, and who is responsible for fixing them

November 12, 2026 · Kirkland Dispatch, issue 8 · Every answered question

A reader asked why Kirkland streets get patched so poorly after builders dig them up for utility hookups, and who is responsible for fixing them. The company that dug is. City code bars anyone from cutting a street, sidewalk or curb, or placing a utility in it, without a Public Works right-of-way permit, and the city's utility policy makes the utility company or contractor responsible for the repair: a pavement patch stays under warranty until the city itself overlays or rebuilds that pavement, and a failing patch can make the utility responsible for a larger area. City inspectors check backfill, compaction and repaving, and a long trench can require a half-street or full-street overlay. If a patch near you is sinking or cracking, report it on the Our Kirkland app with a photo and the spot on the map; urgent road hazards go to 425-587-3900, staffed around the clock.
