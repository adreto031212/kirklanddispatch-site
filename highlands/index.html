The latest in Highlands

November 5, 2026

Nothing new this week names Highlands on the city's news or calendar. The November 4 hearing on the capital plan covers the citywide list. Council calendar

October 29, 2026

Highlands Park, 11210 NE 102nd Street, sits where the Highlands water tower stood. The tower went out of service in the 1960s and came down in March 1984; its concrete pads are still there. The 2.7 acres hold a playfield, a playground, basketball and an AED added in 2025. Open 5 AM to 10 PM, street parking. City of Kirkland

October 22, 2026

Cedar View Park, 11401 NE 90th Street, is a 0.2-acre pocket park with a playground, swings and a picnic table, open 5 AM to 10 PM. Street parking only, no restroom. City of Kirkland

October 15, 2026

The city's Arbor Day tree planting is today, Thursday, October 15, at 1 PM at Fire Station 21, 9816 Forbes Creek Drive, with the mayor's proclamation read on site. City bulletin

October 8, 2026

On September 15, council approved a long-term approach to water pressure in the Highlands. The temporary booster station at Forbes Creek Park has held the upper part of the neighborhood at its former pressure since June 23. City bulletin · Highlands water pressure

October 1, 2026

Juanita Bay Park's east entrance, 10442 Forbes Creek Drive (not the main lot), hosts a stewardship morning Friday, October 9, 10 AM to noon: invasive removal, planting, mulching. All experience levels; register on Eventbrite. Eventbrite via City of Kirkland

September 24, 2026

Worth a walk this month: Cotton Hill Park at 11006 NE 98th Street, a 2.2-acre natural area with a gravel interpretive path and a trail link to the Cross Kirkland Corridor. Green Kirkland has been restoring it since 2008. City of Kirkland

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Kirkland newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Kirkland that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Kirkland's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
