The latest in Lakeview

November 5, 2026

Nothing new on the city's news or calendar names Lakeview this week. The November 4 budget hearing is the citywide item to watch. Council calendar

October 29, 2026

Doris Cooper Houghton Beach Park, 5811 Lake Washington Boulevard, carries the name of Kirkland's first woman mayor. In 1969 she, Judy Frolich and Delores Teutsch ran a telegram campaign to Congressman Tom Pelly for federal money to buy the shoreline from two oil companies, and it worked. The seasonal restrooms run March through October. City of Kirkland

October 22, 2026

Marsh Park, 6605 Lake Washington Boulevard, is 4.1 acres on the water with a dock, a hand-carried launch for kayaks and paddleboards, an interpretive trail and year-round restrooms, open sunrise to 10 PM. The land was given by Louis Marsh, a Boeing engineer. City of Kirkland

October 15, 2026

Yarrow Bay Wetlands, 9830 NE Points Drive, is 73.3 acres of wetland with an interpretive walk; trailheads sit at NE Points Drive and 101st Way NE, and off Lake Washington Boulevard NE about 500 feet north of NE 38th Place. Street parking is limited. City of Kirkland

October 8, 2026

Marsh Park, 6605 Lake Washington Boulevard, is 4.1 acres with a lawn, a dock, a hand-carried boat launch and year-round restrooms. The land was given by Louis Marsh, an early Boeing engineer whose father settled near the site in 1905. City of Kirkland

October 1, 2026

The Emerson campus at 10903 NE 53rd Street is being rebuilt and enlarged: a new building for 345 students, ground broken March 26, opening planned for fall 2027. The district posted a September construction update. LWSD project page

September 24, 2026

Doris Cooper Houghton Beach Park, 5811 Lake Washington Boulevard, reopened for water activities on September 15 after a short closure; its seasonal restrooms stay open through October. City of Kirkland

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Kirkland newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Kirkland that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Kirkland's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
